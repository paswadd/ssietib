Leadership & Governance

A clear institutional structure that keeps authority accountable, decisions documented and resources directed to SSIETIB’s mandate.

Section 01

The General Assembly

The General Assembly is the Bureau’s highest deliberative authority. It brings together founding and duly admitted members, institutional partners and representatives of the constituencies SSIETIB serves. The Assembly meets at least once a year and may convene extraordinary sessions when the Governing Board or the required number of members so requests.

The Assembly defines the institution&rsquos democratic foundation: it sets the broad direction, decides on membership and holds the Governing Board accountable for the stewardship of the Bureau.

Core responsibilities

  • ◆ Approve the constitution and any amendments.
  • ◆ Elect and, where necessary, remove Governing Board members.
  • ◆ Receive and approve annual reports, budgets and audited accounts.
  • ◆ Determine membership criteria, admission and disciplinary matters.
  • ◆ Approve the long-term strategy and major institutional partnerships.
  • ◆ Appoint auditors and decide on the dissolution of the Bureau.
Section 02

The Governing Board

The Governing Board is elected by the General Assembly and is responsible for policy, strategy, financial stewardship and oversight between Assembly meetings. Board members act in the interest of the institution and independently of any government, political grouping or commercial interest.

The Board sets the framework within which Executive Management operates, monitors performance and reports back to the membership. It is not a regulator and does not approve investments, issue licences or make binding commitments on behalf of any public institution.

Core responsibilities

  • ◆ Approve policies, the strategic plan and annual work programme.
  • ◆ Approve the annual budget and oversee financial controls.
  • ◆ Supervise Executive Management and review performance reports.
  • ◆ Establish standing and ad hoc committees and receive their reports.
  • ◆ Ensure legal, ethical and conflict-of-interest compliance.
  • ◆ Represent the Bureau in institutional matters and report to the Assembly.
Section 03

The President and Chairperson of the Board of Directors – South Sudan

The President and Chairperson provides strategic leadership of the Bureau, chairs meetings of the Governing Board and represents SSIETIB in institutional engagements, subject to the authority of the Board and the General Assembly. Based in South Sudan, the President focuses on the home-country mandate: building trusted relationships with local enterprises, business associations, development partners and lawful institutional counterparts.

Key responsibilities

  • ◆ Chair Board meetings and ensure sound decision-making processes.
  • ◆ Provide strategic direction consistent with Board-approved policy.
  • ◆ Represent the Bureau at high-level economic and business forums.
  • ◆ Oversee implementation of Board decisions across all offices.
  • ◆ Support responsible resource mobilisation and partnership development.
  • ◆ Safeguard the Bureau’s independence, neutrality and institutional status.
Section 04

The President – China Office and Key Areas of Engagement

The President of the China Office leads SSIETIB’s engagement with Chinese investors, manufacturers, financial institutions, trade associations and economic organisations. The Office is a business facilitation and promotion presence; it does not exercise governmental, consular or diplomatic functions and does not represent any government.

Key Areas of Engagement

Investment Attraction & Investor Servicing

Introduce lawful opportunities to credible investors and guide them through enquiries, due diligence and partner identification.

Trade & Export Development

Promote South Sudanese products to Chinese buyers and support export readiness, standards awareness and market entry.

Business Matchmaking & Partnerships

Organise B2B introductions, joint-venture discussions and institutional alliances with due regard to confidentiality.

Market Intelligence & Research

Provide verified sector information, investment guides and briefings that reflect both opportunities and operational risks.

Trade Missions & Events

Coordinate delegations, investment forums, exhibitions and roundtables between the two business communities.

Institutional Cooperation

Work with chambers, associations, professional bodies and development organisations, distinguishing confirmed agreements from prospective relationships.

Section 05

Executive Management and Secretariat

Executive Management is responsible for the day-to-day administration of the Bureau and for implementing the decisions of the Governing Board. The Secretariat coordinates programmes, member services, finance, compliance, communications and logistics across the Juba headquarters and the China Office.

Core functions

  • ◆ Implement the approved strategy, work programme and budget.
  • ◆ Manage staff, consultants, offices and administrative systems.
  • ◆ Maintain the member register, institutional records and archives.
  • ◆ Organise trade missions, events, forums and training activities.
  • ◆ Prepare reports for the Board, the General Assembly and partners.
  • ◆ Manage enquiries, protect confidential information and uphold data protection.
  • ◆ Provide secretariat support to all Board and committee meetings.
  • ◆ Monitor compliance with policies, donor requirements and applicable law.

Section 07

Standing and Ad Hoc Committees

Committees allow the Board to draw on focused expertise and to supervise specific areas of work. Standing committees continue across terms; ad hoc committees are formed for a defined purpose and stand down after reporting to the Board.

Standing committees

Executive Committee

Coordinates Board business between full meetings and prepares the agenda for Board consideration.

Finance, Audit and Risk Committee

Reviews budgets, accounts, internal controls, audit findings and institutional risk.

Membership and Ethics Committee

Oversees admission, member conduct, conflicts of interest and the code of ethics.

Programmes and Events Committee

Guides the work programme, trade missions, investment forums and training activities.

Research and Publications Committee

Reviews research quality, source standards and the accuracy of published information.

Ad hoc committees and task forces

Investment Promotion Task Force

Reviews investor facilitation procedures and the quality of opportunity listings.

Trade Mission Steering Committee

Plans delegations, confirms participants and oversees mission reporting.

Annual Report Editorial Committee

Assures the accuracy and balance of the Bureau’s annual reporting.

Governance Review Committee

Reviews constitutional compliance and recommends governance improvements.

Special project committees

May be established for specific partnerships, events or investigations, with a defined mandate and term.

Section 08

Our Commitment to Good Governance

SSIETIB is committed to the highest practical standards of institutional governance. Our structures exist to keep authority accountable, decisions documented and resources used for the purposes for which they were entrusted. Good governance protects our independence and the confidence of everyone who works with us.

Details of current office-holders, Board members and committee members will be published as appointments are formally confirmed and authorised. In line with our legal positioning, the Bureau does not claim governmental authority, regulatory power or official diplomatic status.

  • Transparency

    Publish our mandate, structures and procedures, and distinguish facts from proposals.

  • Accountability

    Report regularly to the General Assembly and to partners on performance and finances.

  • Independence

    Keep institutional decisions free from political, governmental or commercial capture.

  • Separation of roles

    Distinguish oversight, management and facilitation, including from official approvals.

  • Financial stewardship

    Apply sound controls, budgeting, procurement and independent audit.

  • Conflicts of interest

    Require disclosure, recusal and registers where appropriate.

  • Inclusive participation

    Ensure diverse voices inform decisions and that opportunities are open and fair.

  • Law and data protection

    Respect applicable law, confidentiality and the privacy of stakeholders.
