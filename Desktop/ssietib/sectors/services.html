Sector overview

South Sudan's services and digital potential

South Sudan's service economy spans banking, mobile money, telecommunications, retail, professional advice and hospitality. Juba is the main commercial centre, with activity expanding in state capitals as trade, development programmes and private enterprise grow. Mobile networks reach urban areas and key transport corridors, and digital payments are becoming part of everyday commerce.

Adoption is still held back by affordability, unreliable power and low digital literacy, leaving clear room for new entrants. Opportunities include fintech, merchant payments, online marketplaces, IT outsourcing, vocational training and business process services. Local partnerships and early regulatory engagement are critical to building trust and reaching customers beyond the formal sector.

Young market

A youthful population drives demand for connectivity, payments, skills and entertainment.

Mobile first

Handsets are the main route to market for payments, information and services.

Urban demand

Juba and state capitals concentrate customers, talent and institutional buyers.

Value chains

Key sub-sectors

Financial services

Banking, microfinance, savings groups, insurance and fintech payment products.

Telecom & connectivity

Mobile networks, broadband, fibre backhaul and shared tower infrastructure.

Digital platforms

Marketplaces, delivery services, ride-hailing and farmer-to-buyer platforms.

Professional services

Legal, accounting, audit, HR and business advisory firms for local and foreign clients.

Education & skills

Vocational centres, ICT academies and corporate training aligned with employer needs.

Hospitality & events

Hotels, restaurants, conference facilities and travel services for business visitors.

Investment focus

Priority opportunities

SSIETIB prioritises service businesses that solve a local constraint, reach customers affordably and can scale across several towns.

  1. 1

    Mobile money and SME lending reaching traders and households outside formal banking.

  2. 2

    Last-mile connectivity and fibre backhaul for businesses, schools and clinics.

  3. 3

    Digital marketplaces linking farmers, traders and buyers with payments and logistics.

  4. 4

    Vocational and ICT training centres serving employers in trade, energy and construction.

  5. 5

    Business process and managed IT services for local firms, NGOs and public institutions.

Facilitation

How SSIETIB supports services investors

We help service providers size local demand, understand regulatory requirements and find credible partners for pilots and national rollout.

  • Market sizing and regulatory mapping for priority service lines.

  • Introductions to banks, telecom operators, regulators and established SMEs.

  • Guidance on licensing, data protection and consumer protection requirements.

  • Market intelligence on payment behaviour, tariffs and digital adoption.

  • Investor roundtables, pilot design and partner matchmaking with local firms.
